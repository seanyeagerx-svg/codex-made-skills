# Portrait Style Screening

## Unit and Brief

Separate the client identity source from the style reference. A direction is a coherent combination of lighting, styling, environment, composition, and expression treatment, not an invented name for every photo.

Clarify intended use when it changes selection: personal portrait, business identity, commercial campaign, or another purpose. Translate vague adjectives into proposed observable choices; do not silently freeze one interpretation of "elegant", "powerful", or "natural".

## Sources

Original photographer/editorial/brand portfolios can establish authorship and visual treatment. Curated boards or social posts can discover alternatives but may be reposts. Inspect actual images, not captions alone. Distinguish photography, AI work with reported provenance, and unknown production method.

Use generic style queries. Do not upload a client's face for reverse search or third-party analysis without authorization. Do not infer personality, social status, ethnicity, health, or other sensitive traits from appearance; work from the stated brief.

## Minimum Criteria

| Dimension | Inspect |
| --- | --- |
| Brief fit | Does the visual treatment express the client's stated use and desired impression? |
| Visual quality | Light direction, skin/material detail, composition, color coherence, distracting artifacts |
| Identity compatibility | Is there a plausible route to retain required face/body traits without copying the reference person's identity? |
| Styling coherence | Wardrobe, hair, makeup, pose, props, and setting work together |
| Practical feasibility | Supplied source quality, required assets, retouching effort, generator or photography constraints |

Source-image usability and final identity fidelity are different questions. If the client image has not been inspected, say suitability is provisional. A text promise such as "100% same face" does not validate identity preservation. Do not assume the user's chosen expression, wardrobe, age appearance, or body shape may be changed.

## Selection and Handoff

- Respect explicit keep/change rules. A same-outfit requirement cannot be offset by a beautiful reference requiring a different outfit; separate the borrowable light/composition from the unusable wardrobe.
- Group visually similar images. If the client chose a single style, show different useful treatments within it rather than unrelated aesthetics.
- Show an actual reference preview when possible, what to borrow, what not to borrow, and the source/permission limits.
- Keep client photos out of public/example packages. No image generation or batch uploading in this screening stage.
- Handoff contains client-stated invariants, approved styling, candidate IDs, and unresolved risks. A real image test and client approval are later validation, not completed by a shortlist.
